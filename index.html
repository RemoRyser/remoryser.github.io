Approach

A mechanistic core

Our models build up from individual traits and metabolism (body size, foraging, movement) to predict how populations, food webs, and whole landscapes work and respond to change. The same bioenergetic core scales from a single patch to a spatially explicit meta-food-web.

About

About the group

Network Ecology is a research group at the University of Bern, led by Remo Ryser. From February 2027 it becomes a division of the Institute of Ecology and Evolution. We study how networks of interacting species and networks of habitat patches in space form, move and change, and what this means for biodiversity and ecosystem functioning.

Our work is theory-led and mechanistic. We build models from individual traits and metabolism up to food webs and whole landscapes, and confront them with empirical data, from grassland experiments to movement data. Current themes include meta-food-webs and dispersal, biodiversity–ecosystem functioning, movement across scales, and the effects of artificial light at night.

The aim is to turn first-principles theory into forecasts that can inform real decisions in a changing world. We collaborate widely and are building a team of PhD students, postdocs and senior researchers (see People).

Projects

What we work on

Biodiversity–ecosystem functioning in multitrophic metacommunities

SNSF Ambizione project, in collaboration with Eric Allan (UniBe)

How does biodiversity–ecosystem functioning (BEF) depend on multi-trophic interactions, eutrophication, and spatial processes? We combine empirical data from a large grassland experiment (PaNDiv) and alpine grasslands with bioenergetic food-web modelling to uncover the mechanisms behind BEF relationships, including trait-based plant-herbivore network structure, the interaction between food-web structure and nutrient availability, the role of space and movement, and how findings scale from experimental plots to real landscapes.

Researchers: Leonardo Espinosa, Remo Ryser  ·  In collaboration with Elisa Thébault, Martin Goßner, Jean-Yves Humbert

sMars — Synthesis of Movement Across Scales

Movement drives species interactions, community composition, and biodiversity patterns across scales, but the geographic scale of biodiversity research rarely matches the scale at which animals actually move. We are developing an empirically informed, unified concept of spatial scale and a mechanistic dispersal model grounded in energy budgets and metabolism, alongside a general movement database.

Researchers: Remo Ryser  ·  In collaboration with Myriam Hirt  ·  sDiv-funded working group

Meta-networks

New project, starting in 2027

Meta-networks develops a general theory of spatially coupled food webs. Building on our meta-food-web models and on the mechanistic, energy-based model of dispersal, it treats the network of habitat patches as an object of study in its own right: how does the structure of the spatial network shape the dynamics, persistence and functioning of the food webs living on it? The project applies network theory systematically to the spatial network and relates its structure to ecological outcomes.

Researchers: Remo Ryser, PhD student (to be recruited)

Past

Artificial light at night (ALAN)

Fieldwork on how artificial light impairs plant-pollinator networks, followed by the EcoLux experiment on the iDiv EcoTron platform, measuring ecosystem-wide effects from soil microbes to plants and invertebrates. This work led to a thematic session at BES 2021 and a co-edited theme issue, "Light pollution in complex ecological systems," in Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B.
